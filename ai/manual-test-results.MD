Test 3: free mode:

Objective: test the extremes to inspect the mechanics

Results: It's clear that there isn't a cap on the affect of the hot fuel break and I think there should be. When pulling all the control rods out, with all 4 pumps on, I was able to get radioactivity over 3500, and fuel temp over 3500 degrees. This instantly causes rad to plummet back down, and the pumps then quickly cool the fuel. So apart from considering a cap on the braking affect of hot fuel, I think reactor temp should be quicker to increase when there is a very large gap. Surely 3500 degree fuel would cause a huge amount of steam in real life, and that would negate the affect of the massive hot fuel brake on rad. Eventually, leaving all control rods out did result in reactor temp building up, and therefore steam. But it took at least 3 large swings in rad and fuel temp for this to happen. I think the shift log should appear here as well as the others, otherwise the player just playing this won't know it exists. Let's have a default message appear at the start from 'Plant Director: Welcome to your induction. It's the late shift and we're expecting a nice quiet night. Keep things stable'. 

Test four: hard mode

Objective: test the events and the mechanics

Results: The water pumps seem to be overpowered, their affects on rad is too affecting, I can leave 1 on when I want rad to spike and turn another on when I want rad to stabalise, likewise i can turn 3 on when I want rad to plumit. I like the affect of no pumps on, but I feel we should consider weaking the impact of the pumps on reducing rad. I can see the affect when I add 3 pumps is so strong, it really wipes out the steam, which is of course why rad tumbles when 3 pumps is on, let alone 4. I wonder if the impact of pumps on steam should be limited to when there is no pumps on vs when there is at least 1 pump on. After that, when there is at least 1 pump on, pumps affect temperature only and they do so slower than the do currently. Perhaps that change will mean the player doesn't use them as I use them now, toggle off and on to manage power and rad. I am also finding that the control rods are too powerful. I don't really need to use them, but when I do, moving one is more than enough to have the desired affect on rad: it either plummits or it shoots up depending on how much I've moved that one rod. With rad very high, AZ-5 is able to bring it right back down again very quickly.
