Test one: easy mode

Goal: try and survive the 15 mins, notice the mechanics, test the new functionality

Feedback:

UI: in general the changes look great, I love the paper readout chart and the camera affects placed on both reactor images. I noticed some unnecessary text: the control rods modal has text about 18 seconds to insert. This should be removed. Likewise additional text added to water pumps modal regarding 'takes a few seconds to stop' can be removed. On wide screens the reactor cam below the reactor controls isn't inline with the central reactor controls, the paper readouts pushes it to the left. I like the reactor image being central (keep the camera affects), and push the paper readout section to the right of it. For more narrow screens leave it as is. On the homepage I like the black flickering background behind the H1, but the newly added text should be removed. It should be just 'RBMK' as the H1 and 'Nuclear reactor control simulation'

Mechanics: I can see the water pumps are now much more powerful. I found that I was able to reduce 2 or 3 rod insertion a bit and then pretty much leave the control rods unchanged, as updating the water pumps from 3 on to 2 on resulted in a jump in radioactivity, and vica versa. So the water pumps end up being the main controls. I'm not sure this is ideal, the water pumps (or the resulting steam changes) may be over powered. Within the same game I noticed the power target had been increased to 7900 and I was under target with 2 pumps on. I turned 1 pump off, steam (or power at least) shot up quickly, but somehow radioactivity didn't seem to increase. In fact it dropped slightly. When power output shot through the target range I  turned the second water pump back on and radio activity dropped fast. I noticed there was a big gap between fuel temp and reactor temp. Fuel temp was 657, reactor temp was 434. I hadn't causes a large spike or drop in radioactivity so I was surprised to see such a gap between the two figures. Yes I can see that changing the water pump from two to one results in fuel temp without any jump in radioactivity. Fuel temp changes should be pegged to radioactivity.


Test two: free play

Goal: test extremes

Feedback:

Mechanics: I pressed AZ-5 and it worked well. Radioactivity quickly dropped. I turned all pumps off and as a result, both temperatures and steam stayed pretty static. That makes sense but I would expect it to drop slowly given there is no source of heat keeping things hot. I then turned all pumps off and I was surprised how slow both temperatures were to drop. Maybe it makes sense, but with cold water flowing through the reactor I'd expect the cold water to take away the heat quicker. Then I set all control rods to ~10% insertion, waiting to see the impact of the cooling fuel temp. Then the reactivity spike happened, it jumped from 0 to 1200 odd in a few seconds, which is pretty cool if a little extreme, then it plummeted 250 odd, and fuel temperature was suddenly over 1100, with reactor temp over 100. And again another huge swing in radioactivity. The camera affect at high radioactivity levels is great. But these mechanics are wild. Surely fuel temperature needs to take longer to increase? Also the 'hot fuel' break is too strong. I was able to leave the control rods nearly all out, with fuel temp hot, radioactivity around 500 and no issue, this is of course due to the reactor temp lag meaning no rapid steam buildup or meltdown. The fuel hot brake is too strong, and the rate at which fuel temp is able to change is too rapid, and tbh the radioactivity spike is pretty crazy as well, but let's not change too many things at once here. I like the exponential element to it. When reactor temp started to climb higher despite the pumps (which makes sense due to the high fuel temp) I tried turning the 3 out of 4 pumps off, as expect steam and reactor temp increases were seen, but just before meltdown I was able to bring everything back by pressing AZ-5 and turning on the water pumps. So more evidence that the pumps are overpowered
